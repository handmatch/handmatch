Safety & vetting

Trust is the whole job.

Letting someone into your home, or trusting them with your children, is a big decision. Here’s how our team vets every person we recommend, and the simple steps we suggest every family takes too.

How we vet every pick

1

A real conversation

Our team interviews every candidate by phone or video: their experience, how they work, and the kind of home they’re looking for.

2

Identity check

We confirm each candidate is who they say they are, matching their name to a valid government-issued ID.

3

Background check

With the candidate’s written consent, we order a background check through a third-party screening provider, including criminal records and sex offender registry searches.

4

Reference calls

We speak with past employers or families wherever possible, to hear how they work and why they moved on.

We share each result with you before anyone starts in your home. If something doesn’t feel right, we simply don’t recommend that person.

What a background check can, and can’t, tell you

A background check is an important step, but it isn’t a guarantee. It can only find records that have been reported, and what’s available varies by state and county. It tells you about the past, not the future. That’s why we pair it with interviews and references, and why your own judgment matters most.

Your safety checklist

Whoever you hire, through us or anywhere else, these steps help you hire with confidence.

  • Meet before you decide. Start with a video call, then meet in person. Ask about past roles, how they handle tricky moments, and what they need from you.
  • Call the references yourself. A five-minute call with a past family tells you a lot.
  • Try a paid trial. A paid trial day or week lets you both see if it’s the right fit.
  • Put it in writing. Agree the schedule, duties, hourly rate and time off in a simple written agreement.
  • Pay on the books. Pay at least your state’s minimum wage. If you pay a household employee above a set amount in a year, you may owe household employment taxes; see IRS Publication 926.
  • For nannies: share the essentials. Emergency contacts, allergies, medications and house rules, written down in one place.
  • Trust your gut. If it doesn’t feel right, tell us. Every hire includes up to 3 replacements within 90 days at no extra charge.

Respect for the people we place

Safety goes both ways. Helpers never pay handmatch to find work, and we ask every family to agree on fair pay, at or above minimum wage, and a respectful, safe place to work.
